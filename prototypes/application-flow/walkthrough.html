The model returns a third state — possible clash — which the design has to render, and which somebody at Pharos has to retire by publishing dates.

Step 05

The Statement of Faith discloses, it does not gate

The mother disagrees with one article and writes why. The application still submits. What is stored is who answered, what they answered, the free text, the day, and the version of the Statement they were shown — not whether they scrolled it. A scroll-to-unlock control is a WCAG 2.2 AA hazard that buys no real evidence.

Step 06

$550 now, and an unanswered question about the rest

What the family owes the instructors is not — the published material never says whether the $100 deposit comes off the course price or sits on top of it. $1,300 or $900. The flow cannot state a family's total until Pharos answers.

Step 08

Three weeks pass, and the tally quietly goes wrong

Nobody acted. Ballard's cheque went overdue on its own. Then the same family applied twice — and Whitmore now appears twice in Algebra 1, twice in Spanish, twice in both blocks. That tally is the number Jill uses to decide whether a class runs. Deduplication belongs in the tally, not in a small tag on the inbox.

Three things the walkthrough settled: clash detection must compare shared weeks, not shared hours; applied and paid are separate states before Vanco exists and stay separate after it arrives; and the empty payment slot holds. Two it opened: the class tally double-counts a duplicate application, and the deposit’s relationship to tuition is undefined in the school’s own published material.
